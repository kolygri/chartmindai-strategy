Questions 3 + 4Right to win + moat

Defensibility

The moat is approved workflow memory—not skills alone.

Models, MCP and prompt skills are inputs. Defensibility starts when cross-customer questions, semantic contracts, edge cases, evaluations and approved histories compound faster than a team can maintain them itself.

Compounds with use

  • Versioned question patterns, metric definitions and source mappings
  • Saved workflows, signed-off reports and explanations
  • Reusable exceptions, reconciliations and golden evals
  • Feedback and approval history that improve adoption

Necessary, not a moat

  • MCP, charts, PPTX, prompt templates and generic skills
  • Azure hosting, encryption, SSO and billing
  • A generic semantic layer or audit log alone

Key risks

The direction is clearer. The specific wedge is not.

Treat these as the four fastest ways to falsify or redirect the thesis.

01

The vertical wedge is unproven

Generic AI claims are ignored and large enterprises often buy late. The ARR pack still lacks matched demand evidence.

Test: recruit through warm, workflow-specific champions.
02

Arbitrary-query trap

Unrestricted language over ambiguous data creates long clarification and validation work.

Test: begin with 10–20 recurring questions, then expand from usage.
03

Metadata becomes services

Weak definitions, unstable schemas and customer rules can pull the team into bespoke cleanup.

Test: score metadata readiness and reusable core separately.
04

Security fractures delivery

Per-customer hosting, credentials and upgrades can erase SaaS economics.

Test: export-first, then one customer-controlled deployment pattern.
